The problem

Everyone talks to AI all day. All of it happens in a chat box.

⏳

Agents run in the background. You can't tell when they need you.

People now run several agents at once (coding, research, email). They keep switching windows to check what's done, what's stuck, and what's waiting for approval.

🧾

Getting physical things into AI is clunky.

Receipts, whiteboards, sketches, files on a coworker's phone. Each one means photographing, uploading, and copy-pasting before the AI can even start.

⚠️

Clicking "Approve" is too easy.

Agents can now send emails, change files and move money. One careless click on a dialog box can't be undone.

Why not just another gadget? Humane AI Pin and Rabbit R1 tried to replace the phone and lost. Nibble doesn't replace anything. It's the body for the AI tools you already use. It does what a screen can't: it stays in your peripheral vision, takes physical things, gives you paper, and makes you decide with your hands.

Input · 02

Tap your phone on its nose

Nibble's nose has an NFC tag that holds a link. Tapping it opens the Nibble app straight to an upload page that already knows which robot you tapped. NFC only does the handshake, and the file itself goes over Wi-Fi, so size isn't a problem.

  1. Drag the phone onto the orange NFC nose, or tap the phone.
  2. Pick something on the upload page. Nibble reacts differently to photos, PDFs and links.

With iPhone App Clips, a coworker or visitor can tap and upload without installing anything.

Decide · 06

You decide, with your hands

The riskier the action, the more deliberate the gesture. Interact with Nibble's head directly:

  • Pat (click the head) → approve
  • Hold 3 s (high risk) → the ring fills up, then it runs
  • Push away (drag the head sideways) → reject
  • Pat its back (click the body) within 10 s → undo

    Risks we're watching

    📉 Novelty wears off

    Jibo and Anki struggled with exactly this. Our answer: tie Nibble to daily work, not just cuteness.

    🏭 Hardware is hard

    Manufacturing, QA, shipping. Start with small batches and a pre-order page.

    🐘 Big players

    OpenAI and others are exploring devices. We focus on a niche they won't build for: agent-heavy desks.

    💸 Model costs

    Per-user monthly API cost has to fit inside the subscription. Bring-your-own key helps.

    The one metric

    Do people still talk to it on day 7?

    Validation plan: put 5–10 prototypes on classmates' desks for a week. Track how many times per day they feed it, pat it, or print. Launch a 30-second video and a pre-order page to measure demand.

    1. Wk 1–2 ESP32 prototype: talks, has eyes, turns its head
    2. Wk 3–4 Connect Claude Code status + approvals
    3. Wk 5 30 s demo video + pre-order page
    4. Wk 6+ 5–10 desks for a week → measure day-7 use
